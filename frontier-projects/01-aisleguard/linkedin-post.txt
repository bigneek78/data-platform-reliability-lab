AisleGuard: Robot Route Replanning

How can a simulated warehouse robot find a short route after an aisle closes?

I’m sharing a self-contained Project Phoenix prototype with working code, eight local tests, a reproducible synthetic fixture, an architecture diagram, and an animated execution trace.

The demonstration reaches the destination in 11 steps both before and after the closure. Equal route length does not mean equal route: the trace avoids the newly blocked cells. Tests check adjacency, blocked endpoints, unreachable goals, malformed grids, and a known four-step route.

Engineering judgment: A* is appropriate for this discrete, uniform-cost grid. It does not model turning radius, localization uncertainty, moving people, or braking distance. Replanning starts from the fixture origin; it does not pretend to recover a physical robot pose in motion.

Built with AI assistance. These are synthetic prototype results, not production or employment outcomes.
